# subject predicate comma

Remove the comma in “all that we have any business to know, is” in chapter 38 and the comma before “attracted her attention” after the long umbrella subject in chapter 46. In both sentences the comma separates the subject from its predicate. Removing it yields a smoother modern reading, while the existing rhetorical pause remains intelligible. These are discretionary revisions with medium confidence; the supplied general style and exception clauses do not establish a specific mandatory comma prohibition.
